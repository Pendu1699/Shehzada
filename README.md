# Shehzada · ਸ਼ਹਿਜ਼ਾਦਾ

A browser-based, turn-based grand strategy game of the Punjab from the formation of
the Dal Khalsa (Vaisakhi 1748) to the annexation of 1849. Lead a Sikh misl through a
century of fire, keep the two swords of **Miri** (temporal power) and **Piri** (the
Panth's trust) in balance, and hear a dhadi sing the vaar of your century.

Everything you see and hear is generated in code: the wasli-paper map, the painted
figures, the drone, drums and sarangi. The only bundled third-party assets are
open-licence fonts installed through `@fontsource`.

## ▶ Play now — no download needed

**Click here: [pendu1699.github.io/Shehzada](https://pendu1699.github.io/Shehzada/)**

That's it. The game opens in your web browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox) on a laptop
or desktop computer. Nothing to install, nothing to sign up for. Your games are saved in that

> It works best on a computer with a mouse. Phones and tablets work, but the map is small.

## How to play (for complete beginners)

**You are the leader of a Sikh misl** — a band of horsemen — in the Punjab in the 1700s. Over
a hundred years you try to survive, grow strong, and keep the trust of the Panth, until the
story ends in 1849.

**Starting a game**

1. Click **Begin a new vaar**.
2. Pick a starting year. For your first game choose **1748**.
3. Pick a misl. For your first game choose **Ahluwalia** (it says "Historic path").
4. Click **Ride out**.

**The old man will teach you.** A box at the bottom of the screen — the counsel of Nawab
Kapur Singh — tells you what to do, one small step at a time, and the button or place he's
talking about glows orange. Do what he says, or click **Next →**. You can bring him back any
time with the orange **? Counsel** button at the top of the screen.

**What you do each turn**

1. **Move your army.** Click on your horsemen, then drag them onto a nearby area of the map
   (or right-click the area). A red line shows where they'll ride.
2. **Look after your land.** Right-click one of your areas to open a free kitchen (*langar*),
   strengthen a fort, or raise more men.
3. **End the turn.** Click the big drum (**Beat the nagara**) in the bottom-right corner.
   Everyone moves, battles happen, and a storyteller tells you what happened.
4. Sometimes news arrives with a choice to make. Read it and pick what you think is right.

**The two numbers that matter** are at the top: **Miri** (your power — land, money, soldiers)
and **Piri** (how much the Sikh community trusts you). Try to grow both together. Being
generous, keeping promises and sparing cities raises Piri; cruelty and greed lower it.

**What's happening elsewhere?** Click **Khabar** (or press **N**) for the season's news from
all over the subcontinent, written like an old court newsletter.

**Unifying the Punjab.** The orange **Unify the Punjab** button shows what it takes to found the
Sikh Empire. It's meant to be hard: you need a lot of land and both Miri and Piri high.

**Growing your state.**
- *Reforms* (top bar, or **R**): a tech tree in three branches: the army, the treasury and the people.
  Pick one at a time; it costs silver and takes a few seasons.
- *Your lands:* click one of your regions to set its **tax** (light, normal or heavy), **grow its town**
  from a village up to a great city, and build roads, markets, canals, schools and places of worship.

**Alerts and puppets.** A red card drops in whenever someone declares war on you. In the
**Bahi-khata → Diplomacy** page you can **Make puppet** of a much weaker neighbour (or an enemy you're
beating): it keeps its lands, fights beside you and pays the tribute you choose (10, 20 or 35%).
Heavy tribute makes it restless — at full liberty desire it revolts. After 10 seasons you can annex a loyal puppet.

**Asking for silver.** In **Bahi-khata → Diplomacy**, **Ask silver** asks a richer friend for a subsidy.
Allies and houses that like you give more readily; each can be asked once every two years.

**Unions.** In **Bahi-khata → Union** you can found a named league and invite other houses. Members
are allies, ride to war when any one of them is attacked, let you raise men in their lands, and
accept silver from you to build their own armies.

**Map views.** Buttons at the bottom-left switch the map between **Painted**, **Countries**,
**Alliances**, **Terrain**, **Faith** and **Wealth** (and **Support** in the Long Struggle), each with a small legend.

**The Dal Khalsa and the Akal Takht.** In the early starts every misl except the Phulkian houses
rides in the **Dal Khalsa**: when one member is attacked by an outsider, the others usually come. It
holds until **1799** (or until someone founds the Sarkar), then breaks apart, and after that every
house is on its own. At the Akal Takht sits a **Jathedar**. While Sikhs hold Amritsar he is a Nihang
of the Akali tradition — Nawab Kapur Singh, Jassa Singh Ahluwalia, Akali Phula Singh, Akali Hanuman
Singh. If Amritsar falls to Patiala's house or to the British, a government **sarbrah** (a masand-like
manager) is put in charge, and every Sikh house slowly loses Piri while he sits there. If your Piri sinks
too low, an Akali Jathedar may summon you to the Takht for **tankhah** (a penance).

**The Long Struggle (1849–1947).** Pick **1849** on the start screen for a different kind of game.
Punjab has been annexed; you play the **Khalsa Panth**, a movement with no army and no land. Open
**The Movement** (top bar, or **G**) and use your organisers each season:
- click a region of the Punjab to **Organise** support, plant secret **cells**, hold a **morcha**
  (protest), **Lie low** before a raid, or — once support and cells are strong — **Rise**;
- across the province, **set up a press** and print your paper (from pamphlets to the *Ghadar* printed
  in San Francisco), **hunt informers**, or **petition the Crown**;
- **friends abroad**: France, Russia, Kabul, the Irish, the diaspora in America, Germany, Japan — each
  only in its own years. Send envoys until they listen, then **meet** the real people of the time
  (Duleep Singh in Paris, Katkov in Moscow, the Amir at Kabul, Clan na Gael, the Ghadar Party in San
  Francisco, the Berlin India Committee, Mohan Singh's INA…) and ask for **money**, **arms** for a rising,
  **shelter** for hunted men, or for them to **speak for you** to the world;
- **money**: donations and remittances come in each season, and the organisers, the press and lawyers
  for the arrested cost money. Raise more by **collecting funds** at the gurdwaras and melas (Vaisakhi at
  Amritsar, Hola Mohalla at Anandpur, Maghi at Muktsar), and by founding **ventures** that pay every
  season — a trading house, land in the canal colonies, a Sikh bank, farms in California, a motor
  transport company — or Khalsa schools, which train more organisers. The **Funds** tab shows the accounts.
The police watch you (**Heat**). Your own spies may warn you of a raid a season ahead; with too much
heat come mass arrests. History arrives along the way: Maharani Jind Kaur's escape, Duleep Singh's
return, the Kukas, the Singh Sabha, the Ghadar Party and the Komagata Maru, Jallianwala Bagh,
Nankana Sahib and the morchas, the world wars and 1947. The **Support** map view shows where the
movement is strong. Win by bringing back the Raj — or at least freeing the gurdwaras or making the
world hear the Panth before the line is drawn.

**After 1947.** The Long Struggle doesn't have to end in 1947. On the closing page choose **Ride on beyond
1947**: the British leave, the Punjab is cut along the Radcliffe line, and India and Pakistan take over the
Raj's lands. Then:
- **If you hold land** (a rising that held), you sit at the negotiating table. Proclaim a sovereign Sikh
  state — **Khalistan**, **Sikhistan** or the restored **Sarkar-i-Khalsa** — or join India as **Azad Punjab**,
  a self-governing Sikh state. How much land you get depends on your strength at the table: land held,
  Amritsar and Lahore, men under arms, friends abroad, support at home. Strong enough, and the Sikh lands
  around you join; stronger still, and the princes of Patiala, Nabha, Jind, Faridkot and Kapurthala come
  too. Then the game goes on as a state: the refugees of 1947, the Kashmir war, the canal waters, a constitution.
- **If you hold none**, the movement goes on as the **Punjabi Suba movement** (1947–1966): morchas,
  mass arrests, the banned slogan of 1955, the great morcha of 1960, Sant Fateh Singh's fast — for a state
  of the Punjabi language. Win it before history does, or see the smaller Suba of 1966.
If you win the Raj back *before* 1947, you can also ride on and play the restored Raj against the British.

**After 1849.** When the century ends you get your Vaar, and if your house still stands you can
choose **Ride on beyond 1849**: the world carries on, with the computer playing every other power.

**Not sure what something means?** Hover your mouse over it — almost everything explains
itself. The **Codex** button explains the Punjabi words.

**The mouse, in short**

| Do this | And this happens |
| --- | --- |
| Left-click | Select something (it never gives an order by accident) |
| Drag the empty map / scroll the wheel | Move around / zoom in and out |
| Drag your army somewhere | March there |
| Right-click your army | Its orders: split it, join armies, how it fights |
| Right-click land | Langar, fort, recruiting, offering protection |

**Saving, and older saves.** **Save** keeps games in your browser, on this computer; they carry over when
the game is updated (saves from older versions load and play on). To move a game to another computer, or
to keep a backup, use **Save → Export JSON**, and **Import JSON** to load it back.

Settings (the ⚙ button) has bigger text, colour-blind mode, and sound on/off.

## Playing on your own computer (optional)

Only needed if the link above doesn't work for you, or you'd like to play offline.

**1. Install Node.js** — a free program the game needs to start. Go to
[nodejs.org](https://nodejs.org), click the big **LTS** download button, and install it like
any other app (keep clicking Next / Continue).

**2. Download the game** — click
[this link to download the game](https://github.com/pendu1699/Shehzada/archive/refs/heads/main.zip),
then double-click the downloaded file to unzip it. You'll get a folder called `Shehzada-main`.

**3. Open Terminal (Mac) or Command Prompt (Windows)**

- *Mac:* press **Cmd + Space**, type **Terminal**, press Enter.
- *Windows:* press the **Windows key**, type **cmd**, press Enter.

**4. Go into the game's folder.** Type `cd` followed by a space, then drag the `Shehzada-main`
folder from your Downloads into the window, and press **Enter**.

**5. Copy and paste these, one at a time, pressing Enter after each:**

```bash
npm install
```

```bash
npm run dev
```

The first one takes a minute or two (only needed once). The second starts the game.

**6. Open your browser and go to http://localhost:5173** — the game is there.

Keep the Terminal window open while you play. When you're done, close it. Next time, repeat
steps 3, 4, and only the second command (`npm run dev`).

**If something goes wrong**

- *"Could not read package.json"* — you're not in the game's folder; do step 4 again.
- *"command not found: npm"* or *"'npm' is not recognized"* — Node.js isn't installed; do step 1,
  then close and reopen Terminal.
- *An error mentioning esbuild* — type `npm rebuild esbuild`, press Enter, then `npm run dev`.
- Warnings about "vulnerabilities" during step 5 are normal and harmless. Ignore them.

## Design overview

- **The two swords.** Miri (temporal power) is computed from land, revenue, armies and forts. Piri (the
  Panth's trust) only moves through costly choices: langar, sparing cities, keeping rakhi, honouring
  gurmatas, tolerance of every faith. Let Miri run far ahead and your polity grows brittle — commanders
  waver and may betray you in battle.
- **The dhadi.** Every turn is sung as a short vaar passage; the game ends with your own Vaar, generated
  from what actually happened, on an illuminated page you can save as an image.
- **The painted subcontinent.** The map runs from Herat to Bengal and from Ladakh and western Tibet to
  Ceylon. The Punjab is drawn in detail (61 regions) and is where the game is fought; the rest of South
  Asia (84 larger regions: Afghanistan, Sindh, Rajputana, Hindustan, Bengal, the Deccan, the South,
  Nepal and Tibet) follows its own history around you — Plassey, Panipat, Srirangapatna, the fall of the
  Marathas, the conquest of Sindh — unless you hold the land.
- **Four ages, one century.** A game started in 1748 runs on through *The Misl Raj* (1765), *The Lion of
  Lahore* (1799) and *Twilight of the Sarkar* (1839). Each age opens with a page saying what has changed and
  what history would bring next; its name follows your story (*The Bhangi Sarkar*, *The Confederate Khalsa*,
  *The Two Empires*...).
- **Alternative history.** Any misl that holds Lahore and Amritsar with the Panth's trust can proclaim the
  Sarkar-i-Khalsa early — or keep the misls a united confederacy. Ranjit Singh may find Lahore already
  yours. Delhi, Kabul, Patna Sahib, Hazur Sahib, Lhasa and Fort William can all be reached.
- **The Sarbat Khalsa** gathers before the Akal Takht, painted as the era had it. The Dal Khalsa binds the
  misls until 1799; the Jathedar of the Takht is an Akali while Sikhs hold Amritsar, and a government
  sarbrah, draining the Panth, when they don't.
- **History by default, bendable.** Scripted events from Ram Rauni (1748) to annexation (1849) fire unless
  their conditions have been changed — the succession tragedies and the treachery of 1845–46 can be averted.
- **Respect.** The Gurus are never depicted; no Gurbani is quoted or invented; no Sikh faction can
  desecrate a sacred site (enforced and tested).

Five scenarios: 1748 *The Age of Ghallugharas*, 1767 *The Misl Raj*, 1799 *The Lion of Lahore*,
1839 *Twilight of the Sarkar*, and 1849 *The Long Struggle* — a political game of organisers, presses,
spies, morchas and friends abroad that runs to 1947.
